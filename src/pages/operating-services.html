Defined business functions, delivered through AI-native operating models.

Many organisations do not want to own and run another AI system. They want a function to perform better: enquiries answered, documents processed, follow-ups made and exceptions resolved, at a predictable cost.

AI-native operating services are recurring engagements in which Comet takes responsibility for running a defined workflow, combining AI, automation, human expertise and operational oversight within agreed service boundaries. How an operating service works.

Each service is designed around one workflow: what arrives, what must happen to it, who decides what, and what good looks like.

  1. IntakeRequests, documents and data arrive through agreed channels and systems.
  2. AI processingClassification, extraction, drafting, summarisation and routing.
  3. Human reviewSpecialists check, decide or escalate wherever the rules require it.
  4. Delivery and reportingWork completed, recorded and measured against the service definition.
How the components work together
ComponentRole in the service
AIReads, classifies, extracts, drafts and routes work, and flags what it cannot handle with confidence.
Software and automationConnects to the client’s systems, applies workflow rules and keeps an audit trail of every action.
Human expertiseReviews, decides and handles exceptions, sensitive cases and anything outside the agreed rules.
Operational oversightSamples quality, reports performance, maintains controls and improves the workflow over time.

How it differs from traditional outsourcing.

Traditional outsourcing

The existing process moves, largely unchanged.

  • The provider’s advantage is usually the cost of labour.
  • Capacity grows roughly in line with headcount.

AI-native operating service

The work is redesigned before it is run.

  • AI handles defined steps; people add judgement where it matters; controls are designed in.
  • Capacity can grow without a proportionate increase in people, and the workflow improves as it runs.

This does not make it the right answer for every function. Some work is better kept in-house, automated outright or outsourced conventionally, and an assessment should say so.

Four areas of operation.

The technology, level of automation, commercial structure and operating risk differ from one function to the next. Each service is designed for its own workflow.

Lower cost-to-serve, faster responses and more consistent service, with capacity that does not depend on headcount alone.

Functions

  • Case administration and routing
  • Customer communications

Boundaries, quality and accountability.

Service definition

Scope, inputs, outputs, volumes, operating hours and exclusions are written down before work begins.

Quality standards

Accuracy thresholds, review sampling and the rules for escalation are agreed and reported against.

Human escalation

The service defines when and how work passes to a person, and which decisions are never automated.

Audit and data

Every action is logged. Data is handled under agreed terms, and the client retains ownership of it.

Change and exit

How the service changes, and how it is returned or transferred at the end, is agreed at the start.

Assessing the economics.

The economics of an operating service should be visible before it starts.

We compare the current cost and performance of the function with the proposed service, including transition costs and the work that remains with the client’s team. Commercial structures depend on the function: some suit a fixed fee for a defined scope, others a price per unit of work.

Measures typically agreed

  • Cost per unit of work
  • Turnaround time
  • Quality and error rates
  • Volume and peak capacity
  • Escalation and exception rates
  • Backlog and service continuity

Measures and targets are agreed once the baseline is understood. We do not quote outcomes before then.
