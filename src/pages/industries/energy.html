Industries/Energy

Energy

Applying intelligence to a complex energy future.

Energy businesses operate in an environment defined by capital intensity, operational complexity, infrastructure dependencies and long investment horizons. Artificial intelligence is introducing new possibilities for understanding assets, forecasting demand, improving decisions, redesigning work and developing new services.

The strategic challenge is determining where these capabilities offer material value and how they can be introduced without compromising operational reliability, safety or control. Comet works with energy businesses, utilities and infrastructure operators to connect AI strategy with operational priorities and business economics.

Business value in the physical world.

In energy, technology must coexist with physical assets, established engineering disciplines, operational constraints and the requirements of critical infrastructure.

A technically impressive AI system has limited value if it cannot operate reliably within that environment. We begin with the operational and economic question.

Where can better intelligence improve performance? Which workflows can be redesigned? Which decisions would benefit from stronger analysis? What new commercial opportunities are becoming possible?

Our approach connects these questions with appropriate technical and organisational responses.

Areas of focus

  1. Operational Performance

    Examine opportunities to improve processes, reduce administrative and operational inefficiencies, and support more effective use of resources.

  2. Asset and Infrastructure Intelligence

    Assess AI applications that support asset information, maintenance planning, operational analysis and infrastructure decision-making.

  3. Forecasting and Decision Support

    Explore applications in forecasting, scenario analysis, demand planning and operational decision support, subject to data availability and validation.

  4. Enterprise AI Strategy

    Define where AI should be prioritised across business functions, operating assets and enterprise capabilities.

  5. Operating Model Transformation

    Evaluate how AI changes workflows, organisational capabilities, governance and the relationship between technical and business teams.

  6. Energy Innovation and New Ventures

    Explore AI-enabled products, services and new commercial models, including opportunities at the intersection of energy infrastructure, digital services and customer engagement.

Engineering in operationally sensitive environments.

Energy applications often involve complex legacy systems, distributed infrastructure, sensitive operational information and strict requirements for reliability.

We define engineering scope around the relevant technical, security and operational constraints. This may include private infrastructure, controlled networks, restricted data access and integration with established enterprise platforms.

AI systems intended to inform consequential operating decisions require appropriate validation, oversight and safety controls.
