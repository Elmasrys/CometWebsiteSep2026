Work

When intelligence changes, work should change.

Much of today’s work was designed around a particular assumption: people are responsible for interpreting information, coordinating activity and making most operational decisions.

AI changes what is technically possible within that arrangement.

It can help people perform tasks more effectively, automate defined activities and coordinate increasingly complex sequences of work.

But meaningful improvement often requires redesigning the workflow itself.

Beyond task automation.

Automating an inefficient process does not necessarily produce an efficient organisation.

The greater opportunity may be to eliminate unnecessary steps, change how work moves between functions or redesign an entire service around new capabilities.

This requires understanding the process, its dependencies, exceptions, decision points and operating consequences.

Human expertise and intelligent systems.

Not all work should be automated.

Some activities depend on relationships, contextual judgement, professional responsibility or accountability that must remain with people.

Other activities are sufficiently structured to be delegated to software, automation or controlled AI agents.

We examine how these capabilities can complement one another and where responsibility should sit.

Areas of examination.

Workflow redesign
Reconsidering processes from end to end.
Agentic AI
Evaluating where AI agents can execute defined tasks and coordinate activity.
Human and AI collaboration
Establishing effective responsibilities, boundaries and escalation paths.
Operational productivity
Identifying opportunities to improve throughput, responsiveness and cost-to-serve.
Workforce implications
Understanding how roles, skills and management practices must evolve.

The defining question.

If we were designing this workflow today, with AI capabilities already available, would we organise the work in the same way?
