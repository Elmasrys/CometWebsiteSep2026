The Four Fundamentals

A framework for understanding business in the AI economy.

Every organisation must create value, organise work, operate effectively and find ways to grow. These are enduring business requirements.

What artificial intelligence changes is the economics of how they are fulfilled.

Comet’s Four Fundamentals framework provides a structured perspective on the strategic implications of AI across Value, Work, Organisation and Growth.

It helps leadership teams examine not simply where AI can be applied, but where it may require a different answer to fundamental business questions.

Why the framework matters.

AI creates an expanding range of possibilities.

Yet the number of potential applications is not a useful measure of strategic importance. An organisation may identify dozens of opportunities to automate tasks while overlooking a more consequential change in its business model. It may invest in new tools without redesigning the work around them. It may develop technical capabilities without establishing the organisational conditions for their effective use. Or it may focus on efficiency while new forms of growth become possible.

The Four Fundamentals provides a way to step outside the technology catalogue and examine the business itself.

01

Value

{{#images.fundamentals_value.available}}{{/images.fundamentals_value.available}}

{{fundamentals.0.question}}

AI can change the economics of serving customers, the value customers place on different capabilities and the cost of delivering products and services. It can alter margins, pricing models, service differentiation and competitive advantage. The strategic question is whether the current value proposition remains the right one.

Questions worth asking

  • What becomes more valuable or less valuable as intelligence becomes more accessible?
  • Which parts of our offering may become commoditised?
  • Where can AI change our revenue or cost model?
  • What should customers pay for in the future?

02

Work

{{#images.fundamentals_work.available}}{{/images.fundamentals_work.available}}

{{fundamentals.1.question}}

AI creates new possibilities for dividing work between people, software and intelligent agents. The opportunity is not limited to automating isolated tasks. It includes redesigning workflows, coordinating activity across systems and reconsidering where human expertise adds the greatest value.

Questions worth asking

  • Which activities require human judgement?
  • Which workflows can be redesigned rather than merely accelerated?
  • Where can AI take responsibility for defined tasks?
  • What new capabilities will people and teams require?

03

Organisation

{{#images.fundamentals_organisation.available}}{{/images.fundamentals_organisation.available}}

{{fundamentals.2.question}}

Changes in how work is performed have consequences for how an enterprise is structured and governed. AI may alter decision-making, management responsibilities, information flows, investment priorities and organisational boundaries. The organisation must evolve to make effective use of new capabilities.

Questions worth asking

  • Where should decisions be made?
  • Which organisational structures remain appropriate?
  • What governance is required for increasingly autonomous systems?
  • How should accountability, investment and capability ownership change?

Explore Organisation

04

Growth

{{#images.fundamentals_growth.available}}{{/images.fundamentals_growth.available}}

{{fundamentals.3.question}}

AI can reduce the time and cost of developing products, creating services and testing new business ideas. It may change which opportunities are commercially viable and which established organisations are best positioned to pursue them.

The question extends beyond improving today’s business. It asks what the organisation could become.

Questions worth asking

  • Which new markets or services become economically attractive?
  • What can we build that previously required prohibitive resources?
  • Which existing assets create an advantage in developing new ventures?
  • Should the opportunity be pursued within the existing business or through a new venture?

Explore Growth

A strategic perspective, not a mandatory delivery process.

The Four Fundamentals can inform assessments, strategic decisions, executive learning and the evaluation of engineering opportunities.

Not every engagement requires examination of all four dimensions. The framework provides an intellectual foundation for understanding how AI changes the organisation. Its application depends on the specific question being addressed.

What should change?

It is to help leaders recognise the changes that deserve attention and the decisions that follow.
