Organisation

An intelligent organisation needs a different operating model.

Organisations have evolved structures, management processes and governance systems around the capabilities and limitations of people, information and technology. As AI becomes more capable, some of those assumptions deserve reconsideration.

Decisions may be supported by more comprehensive analysis. Work may move across traditional functional boundaries. Teams may gain capabilities that previously required substantial specialist resources. These changes raise organisational questions, not simply technical ones.

Making AI an organisational capability.

Isolated experiments can demonstrate technological potential, but they do not establish an enduring capability.

For AI to contribute meaningfully to performance, organisations need clear strategic priorities, ownership, governance, technical standards and management responsibility. They must also determine which capabilities should be shared across the enterprise and which belong closer to individual business units.

Rethinking decisions and accountability.

AI can influence how decisions are prepared, evaluated and implemented.

But enhanced analytical capability does not remove the need for accountability.

Organisations need to establish where human judgement is required, how automated activity is controlled and what evidence is necessary before decisions or actions proceed.

Areas of examination.

  1. Enterprise AI priorities

    Translating strategic objectives into coherent investment choices.

  2. Operating model design

    Determining the structures and capabilities required for effective execution.

  3. Decision rights

    Clarifying responsibilities between business leaders, technical teams and AI systems.

  4. Governance

    Establishing oversight, risk controls and accountability.

  5. Capability development

    Identifying the skills, leadership practices and organisational resources required.

  6. Portfolio transformation

    Coordinating AI priorities across business units or operating companies.
