How does the organisation run?

An AI operating model defines how an organisation decides where to invest in AI, who owns what, how risk is governed and how successful capabilities move from experiment to everyday operation.

The opportunity

Pilots are easy to start. Transformation is harder. AI creates enterprise value only when organisations can make coherent decisions about where to invest, who owns what, how risk is governed and how successful capabilities move beyond experimentation.

Scaling AI is an organisational question

What is an AI operating model?
It is the combination of structures, decision rights, governance, funding mechanisms and capabilities through which an organisation applies AI. A good one makes AI decisions coherent across the enterprise without slowing every team down.
Who should own AI in an organisation?
Ownership is usually shared by design. The executive team owns priorities and investment, business units own outcomes in their domains, and a central function owns shared platforms, standards and risk. Problems arise when ownership is assumed rather than designed.
How should AI governance work?
In proportion to risk: clear decision rights, standards and controls for high-risk applications, and a fast path for low-risk ones. Governance should protect the organisation without turning into a barrier to action.
Should AI be centralised or decentralised?
Rarely entirely either. Platforms, data, security and specialist expertise tend to benefit from central ownership, while use cases and adoption belong close to the business. Most organisations settle on a federated model and adjust the balance as capability grows.
How should organisations prioritise AI investments?
As a portfolio rather than an expanding list of disconnected use cases: assessed against economic impact, feasibility and strategic fit, funded in stages, and stopped when the evidence is weak. The Fundamentals Diagnostic is one structured way to set those priorities.
What is the role of an AI Centre of Excellence?
A Centre of Excellence provides the shared capability individual teams cannot build alone: standards, reusable components, specialist skills and support for adoption. It should enable the business, not become a queue the business has to wait in.
